How it works

1

An institution registers a document

An issuer uploads a certificate. VeriChain reads it with OCR, runs an AI forgery check, then computes its SHA-256 fingerprint and writes only that fingerprint to the blockchain — never the file itself.

2

Anyone can verify it

A verifier — an employer, a university, anyone — re-uploads the same document, or pastes its hash directly. VeriChain re-computes the fingerprint and checks it against the blockchain record.

3

The result is unambiguous

Because a single changed byte produces a completely different hash, an authentic, unmodified document always matches. Anything edited, forged, or never registered does not — and revoked documents are flagged too.

Privacy by design

The blockchain never sees your document — only its SHA-256 hash, a one-way fingerprint that cannot be reversed back into the original file. Institutions keep custody of the documents they issue; the chain only proves that a specific fingerprint was registered by a specific issuer at a specific time, and whether it has since been revoked.
